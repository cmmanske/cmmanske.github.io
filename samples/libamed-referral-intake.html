Referral intake and routing

How to check a new referral and route it to a partner specialist

Applies toReferral coordinators
OwnerDirector, LibaMed Ltd
Version1.0
ReviewEvery 6 months, and whenever a new treatment corridor opens

Purpose

This guide explains what to check when a new referral arrives on the LibaMed platform, and how to route it to a named specialist at a partner hospital. Follow every step in order. Each check protects the patient, the referring clinician and LibaMed.

Before you start: three rules that always apply

  1. Every case starts with a UK-registered clinician. Patients cannot create a referral themselves. If a patient contacts us directly, ask them to speak to their own doctor and send them the information page for clinicians.
  2. Medical records stay on the platform. Never download, email or print patient records. If a partner asks for documents by email, send them the secure platform link instead.
  3. LibaMed never handles patient money. Patients pay the partner hospital directly. We explain the quote, remind patients of payment deadlines and confirm when payments are received. We never receive, hold or refund patient funds.

Steps

1

Confirm the referring clinician is verified

Open the referral from the Referrals queue. Check that the referrer's status shows Verified. Verification means their registration number has been checked against the public register (GMC for UK doctors).

StopIf the referrer is not verified, do not open the case documents. Complete the clinician verification first, or contact the referrer to ask for their registration details.
2

Check the NHS pathway declaration

LibaMed must never be used to bypass an NHS treatment pathway. The referrer must have declared one of the following:

  • the treatment is not available on the NHS for this patient, or
  • the patient is already outside the NHS pathway (self-funding or privately insured).

The platform blocks specialties that are routinely available on the NHS. However, read the declaration and the clinical summary together: they must tell the same story.

StopIf the declaration is missing, unclear or contradicts the clinical summary, put the case on hold and ask the referrer to clarify. Do not route the case until you have a clear answer.
3

Check the patient's consent

The patient must have agreed to each of the five consent statements separately. Check that all five show as given, with a date.

NoteA patient can withdraw consent at any time. If the case shows Consent withdrawn, stop all work on it and inform the referrer. The platform restricts access to the records automatically.
4

Check the documents are complete

Partner specialists cannot review an incomplete case, and every missing document adds days to the patient's wait. Before routing, check that the referral includes:

  • clinical summary and reason for referral
  • histology and biopsy reports, where relevant
  • imaging reports, and the images themselves (DICOM format preferred)
  • current medication
  • other medical conditions
  • relevant family history
  • treatment received so far, and the patient's current treatment status

If anything is missing, use Request information to message the referrer. Name each missing document, so they can send everything in one go.

5

Route the case to a named specialist

  1. Check that the treatment corridor is open. Corridors marked Closed cannot receive cases.
  2. Choose the partner hospital and the named specialist for the condition. Always route to a named person, never to a shared hospital inbox.
  3. Note the partner's review timetable. For example, multidisciplinary tumour boards usually meet weekly and need complete documents a few days before the meeting.
  4. Select Route case. The referrer receives an automatic confirmation.
6

Follow the case through to handback

  • Treatment plan: when the partner sends the plan, check it includes an itemised quote and a timeline before sharing it with the referrer and patient.
  • Payments: explain the quote to the patient, remind them of deadlines and confirm payments received. The patient always pays the hospital directly.
  • Handback: after treatment, make sure the referring clinician receives a structured clinical summary within 5 working days, so care continues smoothly in the UK.

When to escalate

SituationWhat to do
Any clinical safety concern about a caseRaise it in the clinical governance channel the same day. Do not give clinical advice yourself.
A possible data breach (for example, a document sent to the wrong person)Tell the Director immediately. Some partner agreements require notification within 24 hours.
A patient or clinician complaintAcknowledge it promptly and follow the LibaMed complaints procedure.
A partner asks for money to be paid through LibaMedDecline politely and explain that patients pay the hospital directly. Inform the Director.
